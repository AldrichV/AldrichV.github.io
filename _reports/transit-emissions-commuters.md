## The question

After the pandemic lockdowns ended, global CO₂ emissions rebounded to a record high. In European data, denser populations went with higher emissions, and worldwide, energy demand has grown with population. So we asked: **in our own dense, fast-growing suburbs, where does the energy go, and why?**

## What was done

The project ran in three phases:

- **Phase 1, needs assessment:** we surveyed residents of Vaughan, Markham and Scarborough across a range of jobs, ages and lifestyles. We asked about their main uses of energy, how they travel, and what puts them off public transit. From that we wrote a problem statement and a list of attributes and constraints for any solution.
- **Phase 2, design alternatives:** we reworked the problem statement using restatement and a Duncker diagram, brainstormed solutions, and compared them in an evaluation matrix scored on cost, accessibility, reliability, safety and speed.
- **Phase 3:** we pitched the result as a crowdfunding-style video.

I ended up as the team's de facto leader. When nobody would pick a problem, I put it to a vote, and we chose to work on cutting CO₂ emissions.

**Why they avoid transit.** The most common answers were:

- **Safety:** feeling unsafe, both on board and while waiting.
- **Distance:** no bus terminal or train station near home.
- **Frequency:** buses that don't come often enough.
- **Rush hour:** long commutes at peak times.
- **Length:** trips that depend on buses covering long distances.

A recurring complaint from York University commuters was cost. Getting to campus often means combining TTC, YRT/Viva and GO services, and paying two or three fares.

## What it showed

The survey pointed at the real problem. People weren't driving because they didn't care about emissions. They drove because transit didn't feel safe, reliable or quick enough. Every extra car on the road makes commutes longer, and longer commutes push even more people away from transit. In Phase 2 we restated the problem as:

> "The increase in CO₂ emissions is a major environmental and health concern, and the lack of reliable public transportation is contributing to the problem."

Any solution had to be accessible to people in these areas, cut commute times, take traffic off the roads, and stay within what riders would pay. Our reorganization alternative mapped out how to **streamline YRT, Viva and the TTC**. It would add bus terminals where coverage is thin, add stops on existing routes and extend the subway to growing cities like Markham and Vaughan. It would also run shuttle buses to specific stations, move to articulated buses with smarter seating, and add more bus-only lanes.

The biggest lesson for me was that framing the problem was harder than finding solutions. Our first problem statement read more like a fact than a problem, and fixing it took us longer than coming up with the solutions did.
